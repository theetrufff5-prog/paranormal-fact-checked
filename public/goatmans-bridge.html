CASE FILE / DENTON COUNTY, TEXAS

Goatman's Bridge

BRIDGE VERIFIED / ORIGIN STORY UNSUPPORTED

A real 1884 Texas bridge, an unverified lynching story, and decades of reports about a creature said to wait in the woods.

Assessment published September 2026

VerifiedOld Alton Bridge is an 1884 iron Pratt-truss bridge listed in the National Register.
LegendA Black goat farmer named Oscar Washburn was allegedly lynched here in 1938.
UnsupportedResearchers found no contemporary record confirming Washburn or the alleged lynching.

The real bridge

Old Alton Bridge was built over Hickory Creek in 1884 using ironwork from the King Iron Bridge and Manufacturing Company. It carried the principal route between Denton and Dallas and took its name from Alton, an early Denton County seat.

The bridge entered the National Register of Historic Places in 1988 and later became a Recorded Texas Historic Landmark. After road traffic was redirected, it became part of a hiking and equestrian trail.

The Oscar Washburn story

The most repeated legend describes Oscar Washburn as a successful Black goat farmer known as the Goatman. It says Ku Klux Klan members dragged him to the bridge in August 1938 and threw him over the side. When they looked below, his body had disappeared.

Later versions add a goatlike figure, glowing eyes, growls, footsteps, violent feelings, unexplained touches, and rituals involving knocking, honking, or calling the Goatman's name.

A serious claim requires records

Racist violence in Jim Crow-era Texas was real, but that history alone cannot verify this particular victim and event.

The record search

Denton Public Library reports that researchers searched August 1938 issues of the Denton Record-Chronicle without finding the alleged incident. The library concluded that the name Oscar Washburn appears to have been added as the legend evolved.

No matching death record, police file, identified victim, or contemporary report has been produced in the reviewed sources.

Ghost Adventures investigation

Ghost Adventures investigated Goatman's Bridge during the 2016 Texas special Route 666. The segment featured ritual activity and dramatic reported experiences, but it does not independently verify the Oscar Washburn story or demonstrate a supernatural cause.

Assessment

Old Alton Bridge is a genuine and significant 1884 structure. Goatman stories are established Denton folklore, amplified by tours, online videos, and television.

The reviewed record does not verify Oscar Washburn or the alleged 1938 lynching, and witness stories do not establish a ghost, demon, or human-goat creature. The appropriate status is BRIDGE VERIFIED / ORIGIN STORY UNSUPPORTED / PARANORMAL CLAIMS UNPROVEN.
