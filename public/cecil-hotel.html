CASE FILE / LOS ANGELES, CALIFORNIA

The Cecil Hotel

DARK HISTORY VERIFIED / PARANORMAL CLAIMS UNPROVEN

The hotel’s history contains real deaths, hardship, and connections to notorious criminals. Inflated totals and paranormal retellings go beyond what the evidence supports.

Assessment updated September 2026

Verified in substanceDocumented deaths, Richard Ramirez’s 1985 stay, Jack Unterweger’s 1991 stay, and Elisa Lam’s official cause and manner of death.
Firsthand claimFormer manager Amy Price said she believed about 80 people died during her roughly ten years there, many from natural causes.
UnsupportedThe widely repeated “800 deaths” total and claims that paranormal activity caused the hotel’s tragedies.

The death count

Former manager Amy Price said in the documentary Crime Scene: The Vanishing at the Cecil Hotel that she believed about 80 people died during the roughly ten years she worked there. Contemporary coverage of her account makes clear that the total included people who died from natural causes, as well as suicides, overdoses, and other deaths.

That figure is a firsthand recollection, not a published audit or official lifetime total. It should be attributed to Price rather than presented as independently verified. We found no reliable accounting that supports the much larger claim that 800 people died at the Cecil. Repeating a number does not supply its missing records.

Claim check

“Around 80 in ten years” is Price’s own estimate and includes many natural deaths. “800 deaths” is unsupported by the sources reviewed for this case.

Notorious guests

Richard Ramirez: contemporary UPI reporting from September 1985 said Ramirez stayed at least six weeks at the Cecil. This supports the substance of the familiar claim that he lived there for weeks during 1985. It does not mean crimes attributed to him occurred inside the hotel.

Jack Unterweger: hotel records reported by The Guardian show Unterweger checked into the Cecil on June 11, 1991, and checked out July 2. He was later convicted in Austria of murders that included three Los Angeles women. The victims’ bodies were found at locations elsewhere in the Los Angeles area; the record does not support saying those murders occurred inside the Cecil Hotel.

Elisa Lam

Elisa Lam’s 2013 death generated intense speculation after security video from a hotel elevator circulated online. The official Los Angeles County coroner’s finding was accidental drowning, with bipolar disorder listed as a significant contributing condition.

The video’s unsettling appearance is not evidence of a supernatural cause. Paranormal narratives often isolate ambiguous moments from the medical and investigative record. The official finding does not attribute Lam’s death to foul play or paranormal activity, and this case file does not imply otherwise.

Assessment

The Cecil’s dark reputation rests partly on documented events and partly on accumulation: tragedies from different decades are compressed into one story, uncertain details become definite, and unsupported totals are repeated until they sound archival.

The evidence verifies a troubled history. It does not establish a curse, haunting, or paranormal mechanism connecting the events. The appropriate status is DARK HISTORY VERIFIED / PARANORMAL CLAIMS UNPROVEN.
