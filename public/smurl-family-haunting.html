CASE FILE / WEST PITTSTON, PENNSYLVANIA

The Smurl Family Haunting

TESTIMONY DOCUMENTED / PARANORMAL CLAIMS UNPROVEN

The Smurls consistently described years of frightening activity. The public record verifies the controversy—not a demonic cause.

Assessment published September 2026

Verified factThe Smurl family lived in a duplex, reported disturbances publicly, and worked with the Warrens beginning in 1986.
UnsubstantiatedClaims included odors, sounds, apparitions, movement, and physical or sexual attacks attributed to spirits.
Commercial legacyThe case produced national publicity, a book, a television film, and a loose basis for a later Conjuring film.

The family’s account

Jack and Janet Smurl moved with their family into a duplex in West Pittston during the 1970s. They later reported foul odors, unexplained sounds, moving objects, apparitions, and attacks. Members of the family said the events continued for years despite attempts to obtain religious help.

The Warrens arrived in 1986 and interpreted the claims as several human spirits accompanied by a demon. Their involvement helped turn a local report into a national media story.

Evidence and alternatives

The family’s repeated testimony is evidence that the reports were made, not independent proof of their proposed cause. No publicly reviewed recording or physical specimen demonstrates a spirit or demon. Critics suggested structural, plumbing, environmental, medical, and psychological alternatives, while the family maintained that ordinary explanations were inadequate.

The case was subsequently commercialized in the book The Haunted and a television adaptation. Later screen versions added characters and events not present in the original account.

Assessment

The family, reports, Warren involvement, publicity, and adaptations are documented. The most extraordinary incidents remain allegations without independent verification. The appropriate status is TESTIMONY DOCUMENTED / PARANORMAL CLAIMS UNPROVEN.
