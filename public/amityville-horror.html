CASE FILE / AMITYVILLE, NEW YORK

The Amityville Horror

DARK HISTORY VERIFIED / PARANORMAL CLAIMS UNPROVEN

Six murders are the documented horror. The haunting that followed is a disputed account transformed by books, films, television, and decades of retelling.

Assessment published September 2026

Verified factRonald DeFeo Jr. murdered six members of his family at 112 Ocean Avenue in November 1974 and was convicted on six murder counts.
UnsubstantiatedThe Lutz family reported frightening phenomena during a brief residence, but no reliable independent evidence verifies a supernatural cause.
Fiction and embellishmentBooks and films altered incidents, intensified violence, and added scenes that cannot be treated as historical records.

Fact: six murders

During the early morning of November 13, 1974, Ronald DeFeo Jr. shot and killed his parents, Ronald Sr. and Louise, and his siblings Dawn, Allison, Marc, and John at their home at 112 Ocean Avenue.

The trial record says DeFeo used a .35-caliber Marlin rifle, attempted to dispose of the weapon and other evidence, initially blamed a mob killer, and then admitted the killings. At trial, he acknowledged shooting his family but raised an insanity defense.

A Suffolk County jury convicted him in November 1975 of six counts of second-degree murder. The court imposed six consecutive terms of 25 years to life. His conviction was affirmed, and later efforts did not overturn the finding that he acted alone.

Precision matters

The reviewed court decision says the murders occurred in the “early morning hours.” It does not establish the famous pop-culture claim that every killing occurred at precisely 3:15 a.m.

Fact: the Lutz stay

George and Kathy Lutz purchased the house knowing that the murders had occurred there. They moved in with Kathy’s three children during December 1975 and left about four weeks later. The story is conventionally summarized as a 28-day stay, although sources differ slightly on the exact move-in and departure dates.

That short residence is documented. Their reasons for leaving—and the experiences they later described—are claims that require separate evaluation.

Unsubstantiated:
The haunting

The Lutzes described cold spots, unexplained odors and noises, a slamming door, moving beds, strange substances, apparitions, levitation, and waking at roughly 3:15 a.m. George Lutz continued to defend the core story publicly. His stepson Daniel later described his own experiences in the documentary My Amityville Horror.

These are firsthand accounts, but firsthand does not mean independently verified. No reviewed source provides reliable physical evidence demonstrating that a ghost, demon, or other supernatural force caused the reported events.

Contemporary reporting identified important conflicts: descriptions changed between articles, Jay Anson’s book, and the film; an alleged witness disputed being present; police reportedly were not contacted until after the family left; and published historical, weather, and astronomical details did not consistently match the record.

Disputed: the hoax allegation

William Weber, Ronald DeFeo’s defense lawyer, alleged that he and the Lutzes developed the horror story together over wine and that details from the murder investigation were converted into supernatural incidents. The Lutzes denied fabricating the haunting.

Weber’s statement is relevant but not conclusive by itself: it was made amid a financial and contractual dispute. During related commercial litigation, federal judge Jack Weinstein said the book appeared to be fictional to a large extent. That observation is significant evidence of skepticism, but the proceeding was not a scientific test or a court trial deciding whether ghosts exist.

Later occupants publicly rejected the famous account or reported no comparable activity. The careful conclusion is not that every private experience has been disproved; it is that the published haunting narrative lacks reliable independent corroboration and contains material contradictions.

Fiction: book and screen

Jay Anson assembled The Amityville Horror from the Lutzes’ recorded account and published it in 1977 as “a true story.” The bestselling book and its adaptations combined a real crime scene with an increasingly dramatic supernatural narrative.

Contemporary comparisons found that the reported substance changed color and behavior across versions, violence appeared or disappeared, and the movie altered the priest’s story. Later sequels openly introduced fictional events. These productions can document how the legend evolved; they cannot verify what happened inside the house.

Fact versus fiction

A film labeled “based on a true story” may freely compress time, combine people, invent scenes, and heighten danger. Its images are not evidence for the underlying claim.

Paranormal investigators and television

Ed and Lorraine Warren entered the house after the Lutz family had left. Their March 1976 visit was part of a media gathering involving a New York television crew, psychics, a séance, and infrared photography. The Warrens interpreted the location as demonic, but the gathering was not a controlled scientific investigation and produced no independently replicated paranormal evidence.

The case later appeared in In Search of…: The Amityville Horror, History’s Mysteries: Amityville—The Haunting, Amityville—Horror or Hoax?, My Amityville Horror, Very Scary People, and the four-part series Amityville: An Origin Story. Most were retrospective documentaries or retellings—not new investigations inside the privately occupied house.

Assessment

The DeFeo murders, conviction, house purchase, short Lutz residence, book publication, litigation, and media phenomenon are documented. The alleged supernatural events depend primarily on testimony that was commercialized, contradicted in important details, and never independently verified.

The real Amityville horror was the murder of six people. The haunting remains a famous and culturally influential claim—not an established paranormal event. The appropriate status is DARK HISTORY VERIFIED / PARANORMAL CLAIMS UNPROVEN.
