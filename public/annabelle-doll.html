CASE FILE / CONNECTICUT

The Real Annabelle Doll

OBJECT VERIFIED / PARANORMAL CLAIMS UNPROVEN

A Raggedy Ann doll became a cornerstone of the Warren legend, but the extraordinary claims depend mainly on testimony and later retellings.

Assessment published September 2026

Verified factThe real object is a Raggedy Ann doll that came into the Warrens’ possession after a reported 1970–71 case.
UnsubstantiatedOwners reportedly saw movement, notes, blood, and injuries; no public independent record establishes a paranormal cause.
FictionThe porcelain doll, murders, cult history, and most film events were created for entertainment.

The reported case

A nursing student reportedly received the doll as a gift. She and a roommate later said it changed positions and appeared in different rooms. Their account grew to include handwritten notes, drops resembling blood, and scratches on a friend.

A medium allegedly said the spirit of a child named Annabelle Higgins inhabited the doll. The Warrens rejected that interpretation, arguing that a nonhuman entity was manipulating it. They removed the doll and eventually displayed it in a protective case.

What the evidence shows

The doll exists, and a 1971 newspaper account confirms that the Warrens were publicly associating it with a paranormal investigation. Beyond that, the most dramatic claims lack publicly available contemporaneous documentation. No authenticated moving-doll footage, original notes, medical record of the alleged scratches, or verified record of “Annabelle Higgins” has established the story.

Details also changed across oral accounts, books, and films. That makes the case valuable as paranormal folklore, but weak as proof of a dangerous entity.

Assessment

The object and the Warren connection are verified. The supernatural interpretation remains unsubstantiated. The appropriate status is OBJECT VERIFIED / PARANORMAL CLAIMS UNPROVEN.
