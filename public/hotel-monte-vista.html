CASE FILE / FLAGSTAFF, ARIZONA

Hotel Monte Vista

HISTORY VERIFIED / HAUNTINGS UNPROVEN

A landmark hotel with a well-documented civic history and decades of ghost stories whose paranormal causes remain unverified.

Assessment published September 2026

VerifiedThe community-financed hotel opened on January 1, 1927, and is a recognized historic Flagstaff landmark.
ReportedGuests and staff have described knocks, voices, moving objects, apparitions, and unexplained music.
UnsupportedAvailable records do not establish paranormal causes or confirm key identities, deaths, and crimes in several legends.

A community-built hotel

Flagstaff residents organized the Flagstaff Community Hotel Company in 1926 when the growing city wanted modern accommodations. A community stock campaign raised about $200,000, construction began that June, and the 73-room hotel opened on January 1, 1927.

The name “Monte Vista,” meaning mountain view, was selected through a public contest. The hotel remained publicly held until the early 1960s and is now individually listed in the National Register and recognized within Flagstaff's historic downtown.

Radio and public history

In 1929, broadcaster Mary Costigan moved radio station KFXY into the Monte Vista. The hotel later became associated with Route 66 tourism and hosted entertainers and film personalities working in northern Arizona.

Documented history

Contemporary newspapers, archival collections, and city preservation records establish the hotel's construction, opening, ownership, and civic significance independently of its ghost stories.

Room 305 and the bellboy

The hotel's best-known stories include a rocking chair that allegedly returns to the window in Room 305 and a phantom bellboy who knocks on doors and announces room service. Some versions connect the bellboy story to actor John Wayne.

These are reported experiences and later traditions. They do not identify a testable mechanism, and the available sources do not supply contemporaneous documentation proving that a deceased bellboy or unidentified woman causes them.

Rooms 220 and 306

Room 220 is linked to the “Meat Man,” described as a long-term resident who hung raw meat from a chandelier and later died in the room. Room 306 is associated with two women allegedly killed after being thrown from a window.

These dramatic accounts are widely repeated, but repetition is not confirmation. The reviewed material does not provide names, dates, police files, death certificates, or contemporary newspaper reports that securely establish the central events as usually told.

Other reported activity

Additional stories describe vintage music near the elevator, an elevator attendant, an infant crying in the basement, a dancing couple in the lounge, voices, knocks, and objects moving inside rooms.

Such accounts may sincerely describe what witnesses experienced. Without controlled observation or independent records, ordinary possibilities—including building noise, plumbing, elevators, neighboring guests, expectation, memory, and later retelling—cannot be excluded.

Investigation

The Southwest Ghost Hunters Association investigated the hotel beginning in the late 1990s and published a report in 2002. The group gathered staff accounts, reviewed history, and conducted on-site sessions.

The report is useful documentation of what people said and what investigators attempted. Its observations do not demonstrate that a spirit caused the reported events, and several signature legends lacked matching historical records.

Assessment

Hotel Monte Vista's age, architecture, community origins, radio history, and importance to downtown Flagstaff are well supported. Its haunting reputation is also a real part of local folklore and tourism.

No reviewed source establishes a paranormal cause for the reported activity. Several stories lack the names, dates, and contemporary records needed to verify their historical foundations. The appropriate status is HISTORY VERIFIED / HAUNTINGS UNPROVEN.
